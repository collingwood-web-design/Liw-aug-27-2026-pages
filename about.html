About

Liw Bringelson, PhD CEMT CST

I’ve been a horse person my whole life. People call me Liw — it’s short for Liwana. I spent years studying how people use systems: human factors, ergonomics, social psychology, and engineering. For more than twenty years I cared about helping people work safely, calmly, and well.

Finding Quiet is that same curiosity, now in the barn. I still like a clear problem to solve — and I also listen, with my hands and my eyes, to what your horse is already telling us.

I earned a PhD from Purdue University in Industrial Engineering and cognitive human factors in 1991, then taught at universities in the US and Canada. By 2008 I wanted my work and my love of horses in the same place. Training at Prairie Winds College of Equine Massage covered equine anatomy, pathology, movement, natural horsemanship, massage, and the running of a practice.

I work in southern Ontario horse country — Simcoe and Grey counties, especially the Georgian Triangle (Collingwood, Thornbury, Orangeville). I’m often in the Kitchener–Waterloo–Guelph area too, and I’m happy to set up barn days there.

Continued study

I keep looking for anything that helps me treat the horses in front of me more kindly and more effectively. That has included:

  • Eco-Somatics — Level 1 (Oct 2010), Level 2 (June 2011)
  • Therapeutic Touch — Levels I, II, and III (2008–2010)
  • Cranial Sacral Therapy — Level I (Dec 2008) and Level II (Nov 2009)
  • Natural horsemanship, including Parelli and Irwin — ongoing
