Equine massage

Bodywork helps your horse feel more able — in her body, her mind, and her mood — to do the job you ask of her. If you’d like a little more on how massage supports that, there’s a short brochure (PDF).

Bodies take on stress every day. Once your horse has let go of the tightness from a particular knock or strain, coming back regularly — say, once a month — helps her stay in balance and makes the next injury less likely.

A massage usually lasts about an hour to an hour and a half. Some horses want longer; some are done sooner. I go by how she’s engaging, not by the clock.

Three parts of a session

1

Consultation / assessment

We’ll talk about your horse’s health and history. Then I watch her move — from the front, from behind, and at more than one gait when that makes sense.

2

Hands-on

The hands-on work. I follow your horse’s movement and attention, and we work together on the places that are getting in the way of easy movement.

3

Reassessment

Before I leave, I check what has let go and talk with you about after-care and whether another visit would help.

A quiet, comfortable spot is best — fewer distractions, if we can manage it. Grooming is lovely, but if she’s reasonably tidy before I arrive, we can spend the time on the massage itself.

Finding Quiet Equine provides performance-enhancing treatments to complement regular veterinary care. Always consult your veterinarian for diagnosis and treatment of injuries and health-related issues.

Where Liw works

I work in southern Ontario horse country around the Georgian Triangle (Simcoe, Grey, and Dufferin counties). I’m often in Waterloo and Wellington counties too, and I can set aside barn days there.
